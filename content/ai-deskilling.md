# Impact of Generative AI and Automation on Human Skills
## Core Concepts and Definitions
* Deskilling (Levelling Effect)
- Definition: Work requires a lower level of skill than the original job
- Mechanism: Automation strips job content, leaving dull routine
- Consequences
  - Loss of opportunity to develop or maintain skills (skill erosion)
  - Work resembles an assembly line (proletarianization of clerical work)
  - Reduced autonomy and flexibility
* Upskilling (Skill Enhancement)
- Definition: Automation handles repetitive tasks, leaving higher-level, more complex components for humans
- Effects: Job enlargement, increased responsibilities, requirement for new skills
* Skill Erosion and Decay
- Mechanism: Reliance on automation fosters complacency and erodes competence/awareness
- Consequence: Inability to perform core tasks when the automated system fails or is removed
- Skill decay happens subtly, hidden behind high performance
- Illusion of explanatory depth is amplified: belief in understanding without true capability
## Model of Human-GAI Interaction
* Purpose: Determine conditions leading to deskilling versus upskilling
* Model Components
- Human
  - Domain Knowledge (assessing and refining outputs)
  - Prompting Knowledge (formulating effective inputs)
- LLM (Large Language Model)
  - Proficiency/Capability
  - Accuracy/Limitations (propensity for errors)
- Outputs
  - Quality (accuracy, relevance)
  - Speed
- Task
  - Nature (creative, analytical, procedural)
  - Task Division (split between human and LLM)
* Interaction Phases
- Phase 1: Human decides whether to use LLM support
- Phase 2: Human prompts the LLM
- Phase 3: Human interprets and assesses outputs (accept, refine, or re-prompt)
- Phase 4: Final results execute the task
* Potential Skill Outcomes
- Levelling Effect (Deskilling): AI minimizes the importance of prior human knowledge, boosting novices to expert levels
- Multiplier Effect (Upskilling): AI amplifies existing expertise, increasing the performance gap for high-skilled users
- No Effect: Skills remain unchanged, but speed or quality may improve
## Observed Findings from GAI Studies
* Evidence for Levelling/Deskilling
- Customer Service: AI tool significantly boosted novice productivity; minimal benefit for experts
- Consulting: Stronger productivity and quality improvements for lower-performing consultants
- Programming (GitHub Copilot): Developers with less experience benefited more
- Writing (ChatGPT): Subjects who scored worse initially improved quality more; output often used unchanged
- HTML Generation: Non-programmers using GAI performed as fast as professional programmers
* Evidence for Upskilling
- Sales Agents: AI automated routine lead screening, leaving complex sales engagement for humans, where top agents maintained their skill advantage
* Evidence for Skill Maintaining
- Medical Coding: AI required expert evaluation to rule out false positives, maintaining the value of existing task experience
## Risks and Mechanisms of Skill Erosion
* Automation Paradox: Systems improve efficiency but cause manual skills to quietly rust away (e.g., aviation autopilot)
* The Disappearing Act: Humans perform tasks but stop thinking through them, relying on the machine's "breadcrumb trail"
* Complacency: Relying on pre-validated prompts or successful outputs leads to passive engagement
- Risk arises when the underlying GAI model changes or related software breaks
* Impact on Junior Employees: Fewer opportunities to learn on the job and immerse themselves in the work context
## Organizational Implications and Safeguards
* Implications
- Organizational Temptation: Organizations may hire less skilled workers or reduce training investment if systems deskill jobs
- Loss of Expertise: If levelling occurs, organizations risk lacking experts needed to learn answers to new, non-static problems
- Future Skills Focus: Workers need skills in identifying corner cases and managing specific, less frequent problems
* Safeguards and Mitigation Strategies
- Implement Control Points: Technical and organizational checks to ensure employees remain "in the loop"
- Training: Organize workshops for complex cases or build automation-free training environments
- Audits and Validation: Run periodical audits requiring human justification of outputs
- System Design:
  - Add explanation features to essential systems
  - Design AI to provoke questions and challenge human judgment rather than replace it
  - Allow users to tweak prompts to develop prompting expertise
- Scenario Analysis: Consider alternative scenarios where automation becomes unavailable or environmental changes impact necessary skills
