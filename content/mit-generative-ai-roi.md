# The GenAI Divide Report Takeaways
## Report Overview
*   From MIT NANDA initiative, released August 25, 2025
*   **Core Finding: 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots deliver no ROI**
*   Estimated $30-$40 billion enterprise investment, but only 5% of organizations see returns
*   The gap is termed the "Gen AI Divide"
*   Based on interviews, surveys, and analysis of 300 public implementations
*   The divide is driven by "approach," not model quality or regulation
## Seven Major Conclusions
### AI adoption is high, but disruption is low
*   7 out of 9 sectors show low structural change
*   Exceptions: tech sector and media
*   Example: Manufacturing COO sees faster contract processing, but "nothing fundamental has shifted"
### Big enterprises are leading without much to show
*   Companies >$100M revenue have most pilots and AI staff
*   Lowest rates of converting pilots into scaled projects
*   Mid-market companies move faster
### Few custom AI tools reach deployment
*   Generic tools like ChatGPT are widely used
*   **Only 5% of custom AI tools reach production stage**
*   Custom solutions stall due to integration complexity and lack of fit with existing workflows
*   Internal AI chatbots fail in critical workflows due to lack of memory and customization
### "Shadow" AI use dominates
*   Employees use public GenAI tools (ChatGPT, Claude) for daily tasks, often without IT approval
*   Scale is remarkable: 90% of workers use personal AI tools for work vs. 40% of companies with official LLM subscriptions
*   Users find public tools flexible and immediately useful, but distrust them for mission-critical work (lack of memory, cannot learn/improve, repeat mistakes)
### Integration is key, but so is data separation and protection
*   Users want AI systems that remember, learn, improve, and integrate with existing data/tools
*   Simultaneously need to keep sensitive data safe ("can't risk client data mixing with someone else's model")
### Humans are preferred over AI for complex, long-term tasks
*   AI preferred for quick tasks (drafting emails, basic analysis)
*   Humans preferred 9-to-1 for "anything complex or long-term"
*   **The dividing line is memory, adaptability, and learning capability**
*   Solution: Agentic AI (persistent memory, learning from interactions, autonomous workflow orchestration)
### Workforce impacts aren't material yet, for most sectors
*   Gains are from improved personal productivity and speed, "without material workforce reduction"
*   Most valuable changes in back-office automation
*   **ROI comes from reduced external spend** (eliminating BPO contracts, agency fees, expensive consultants)
*   Caveat: Tech and media expect hiring to drop within 24 months
*   Next 18 months are crucial for solidifying AI vendor relationships and integration
## Why Pilots Stall: The Learning Gap
### Core barrier to scaling is the learning gap
*   Most GenAI systems do not retain feedback, adapt to context, or improve over time
### Top barriers identified by executives and users
*   Unwillingness to adopt new tools
*   Model output quality concerns (paradoxically, users trust consumer tools more than enterprise ones)
*   Poor user experience
### Generic tools win and lose
*   Users prefer generic LLM interfaces like ChatGPT for flexibility, familiarity, and perceived better quality for initial tasks
*   However, these tools lack persistence and contextual awareness needed for high-stakes enterprise work
### The learning gap defines the divide
*   ChatGPT's limitations (forgets context, doesn't learn/evolve) prevent broader adoption for mission-critical work
*   **Agentic AI directly addresses this learning gap** by embedding persistent memory and iterative learning
## Crossing the GenAI Divide: How to Succeed
### Common approaches for success
*   Building adaptive, embedded systems that learn from feedback
*   Focusing on narrow but high-value use cases
*   Deep integration into workflows
*   Scaling through continuous learning
### What enterprises actually want from vendors
*   Trustworthy vendor
*   Deep understanding of their workflow
*   Minimal disruption to current tools (seamless integration)
*   Clear data boundaries and protection
*   Ability to improve over time (learn)
*   Flexibility when things change (adapt)
### Winning playbook for startups
*   **Customizing for specific workflows** (low setup burden, immediate visible value)
*   **Leveraging referral networks** (channel partnerships, peer recommendations, advisor referrals)
### Narrowing window to cross the divide
*   Enterprises are increasingly demanding adaptive systems
*   Emerging infrastructure (MCP, A2A, NANDA) supports agent interoperability and coordination
*   Next 18 months are critical for vendors to establish durable relationships before enterprises lock in systems
### How the best buyers succeed
*   Treat AI startups like business service providers (BPO clients), not just software vendors
*   Demand deep customization and benchmark on operational outcomes
*   Drive adoption from the front lines ("prosumers")
*   **Strategic partnerships are twice as likely to succeed as internal builds** (66% vs 33% deployment success)
### Where the real ROI lives
*   Highest ROI often found in ignored functions like **operations and finance (back-office automation)**
*   Real gains come from replacing BPOs and external agencies
*   Back-office deployments often deliver faster payback and clearer cost reductions compared to front-office tools
### Job impact reality
*   Workforce reductions concentrated in historically outsourced, non-core functions (customer support, admin, standardized development)
*   Technology and media sectors anticipate reduced hiring volumes
*   AI literacy is a fundamental hiring requirement across organizations
## Future of AI
### The Agentic Web
*   Beyond individual AI agents, an **"Agentic Web"** will emerge
*   Autonomous systems will discover, negotiate, and coordinate across the internet infrastructure
*   Enabled by protocols like MCP, A2A, and NANDA for agent interoperability and autonomous web navigation
*   Transforms human-mediated business processes into autonomous, self-optimizing workflows
## Conclusion
*   Organizations that cross the divide: **buy rather than build, empower line managers, select deeply integrated and adaptive tools**
*   The Agentic Web is an emerging, persistent layer of learning systems that collaborate dynamically
*   The window to cross the GenAI Divide is rapidly closing; future success depends on systems that learn, remember, or are custom-built for specific processes
*   Path forward: stop static tools, partner for custom systems, focus on workflow integration
