# Becoming an AI-First Company
## Introduction: Why AI-First Now?
* Defining era for business transformation; AI foundational
* Shift driven by:
  * Mainstreaming large language models
  * Rise of AI agents for complex workflows
  * Increased customer/employee expectations for intelligent systems
* AI is a central competitive differentiator
* Companies embracing AI see exponential gains in productivity, speed, customer experiences
* Leading edge AI adopters saw 37% lift in productivity on average
* 60% of companies expect AI transformation within two years
* AI-native employees will choose AI-embracing employers
* Imperative for companies managing vast unstructured content
* Not just reducing workloads, but reinventing business
## Principles of an AI-First Company
* AI as a Capability Expander
  * Accelerate work in ways not possible before
  * Examples: generate software apps, onboard employees in hours, uncover product breakthroughs
  * Power creative problem-solving, proactive decision-making, intelligent collaboration
  * Achieve what was previously unimaginable
* Human-AI Partnership
  * AI should augment, not replace, human work
  * Goal: accelerate decisions, surface content, amplify employee productivity
  * Allows humans to focus on higher-order thinking, relationship-building, innovation
  * Hiring: seek employees fluent in AI-native tools and workflows
  * Upskilling current employees:
    * Foster culture of experimentation
    * Share best practices (prompt libraries, workshops, newsletters, training)
    * Democratize experimentation across functions
  * Some managerial tasks may become obsolete (quality checks, rule-based decisions)
* AI-Native Design
  * Adaptability, continuous learning, intuitive interaction integral
  * Evolve traditional interfaces and permissioning models for agent-based operations
  * Reengineer workflows for real-time, context-aware decision-making
  * Build interfaces that communicate dynamically with AI agents
* AI Anchors: Data Privacy, Security, Trust, Governance
  * Top concern: data protection (74% of respondents)
  * Built with enterprise-grade compliance, auditability, permission models
  * Guiding principle: "AI agents can't keep a secret" – clear separation between data permissions and AI agent workflows
  * Trust is prerequisite for scaling adoption: transparency, managing bias, preserving user agency/privacy
  * Governance embedded early: align technical deployment with organizational values and regulatory expectations
  * AI governance is ethics, values, enablement, and proactive
* Data as a Strategic Asset
  * Treated not as a byproduct, but as a core strategic asset
  * Unstructured data (contracts, invoices, marketing assets) becomes much more valuable with AI
  * AI agents thrive on large volumes of data and tap into its value
  * Streamline processes: due diligence across documents, identifying trends in surveys, employee onboarding, drug discovery, data extraction from documents
  * Effective strategy: data captured, structured, permissioned, and available in real time
  * Without high-quality data: models underperform, result in data leakage, or provide erroneous responses
  * Invest in unified data taxonomies, governance standards, and systems for freshness, lineage, and context
## Common Pitfalls
* Automating Without Rethinking
  * Simply automating existing tasks reinforces inefficiencies or limitations
  * Real breakthroughs begin with rethinking fundamental purpose and structure
* Over-Indexing on Cost Savings vs. Acceleration & Expansion
  * Prioritizing savings too soon can lead to zero-sum thinking
  * Focus instead on shipping more innovation, improved customer engagement, breakthrough insights, better ways of working
  * Leads to positive-sum thinking and greater adoption
* Absence of Data Readiness
  * Businesses need to structure their unstructured data
  * Inadequate data quality, siloed content, inconsistent tagging severely hamper AI performance
  * Building data maturity must be a prerequisite to AI capability development
* Lack of Strategic Alignment and Governance
  * Piecemeal approaches lead to redundant investments, inconsistent experiences, fragmented data
  * AI should be embedded across product, go-to-market, and customer success functions
  * Treating governance/ethics as compliance checkboxes increases risk; must be strategic pillars embedded from day one
* Underestimating Change Management and Overestimating "Big Bangs"
  * Resistance to change from within the organization is a significant risk
  * Clear communication, targeted training, and visible leadership support are essential
  * Best AI strategies grow organically through iterative deployment, visible early wins, and grassroots innovation
## Recommendations for Becoming AI-First
* Nominate Transformation Leaders and Celebrate Innovators
  * Appoint an AI Transformation Lead at the executive level
  * Involve IT, Digital, Engineering, HR, and Operations leadership
  * Every executive should become knowledgeable about AI
  * Celebrate "AI-First contributors" throughout the enterprise
* Invest in Secure, Scalable AI Infrastructure
  * Ensure access to right AI tools and systems that work securely across existing tech stack
  * Support a range of AI models (e.g., OpenAI, Google, Anthropic) and platforms (e.g., Box AI, Salesforce)
  * Interoperability is key for future options
* Develop a Clear AI Ethics and Governance Policy - Early
  * Ethical considerations (data privacy, model explainability, responsible deployment, lack of bias) embedded from the start
  * Policies must be regularly reviewed to encompass the latest AI technology
* Plan to Upskill the Workforce and Train Everyone on AI
  * Provide AI training on best practices for tool use, prompting, selecting AI Agents
  * Encourage decentralized adoption within appropriate governance and guardrails
  * Organize "AI Days" and deploy prompt libraries
* Develop High-Impact Workflows
  * Design AI-native workflows from scratch, reimagining entire approaches
  * Pilot use cases quickly and learn through iteration
  * Early wins build momentum, uncover challenges, and inform broader rollout
## Identifying High-Value Opportunities for AI Enhancement
* Prioritizing Use Cases
  * Start with foundational deployments (email writing, document summarizing, transcribing) for early success and adoption
  * Focus bulk of energy on transformational AI agent use cases
    * Frequent & repeatable work for better experiences (customer support, sales inquiries, insurance claims, RFPs)
    * High critical thinking work for supercharging innovation (drug discovery, product development, strategic planning)
  * "Holy grail": Work that is frequent and requires critical thinking (e.g., augment code writing, automate contract intelligence)
* Framework for Identifying High-Leverage AI-Aided Work (2x2 matrix)
  * Axes: Level of critical thinking involved vs. Number of times the task is repeated
  * Start with foundational, ultimately prioritize transformational
* AI Agents and Traditional Organizational Responsibilities
  * AI agents can map to various organizational levels, from delivering entry-level insights to making complex management decisions
* Driving the Use of AI Agents
  * IT organizations must become closer to lines of business
  * IT (partnering with HR) will educate employees on AI agent capabilities
  * Functional leaders define business outcomes and partner with IT to create multi-agent approaches
  * Team leaders will leverage prebuilt agents from marketplaces or customize through IT
  * AI budgets (compute/token credits) will become fungible with "cost of labor"
* Shift Across Knowledge Workers
  * Individual contributors will effectively become managers of AI Agents
  * Roles will shift to clearly defining tasks, desired outcomes, and quality-checking agent work
* Examples from Internal Box AI Deployments
  * Marketing: Doubling content output by streamlining drafting, editing, publishing
  * HR: Streamlining open enrollment and candidate onboarding workflows, enabling faster responses
  * Product & Engineering: Creating product specification documents, analyzing code, researching customer data
  * Customer Success: Delivering customer answers and best practices 50% faster with internal product knowledge Hub
  * Legal: Automating contract analysis and eDiscovery, improving turnaround times, reducing litigation costs
  * Sales: Automating RFP/RFI responses (saving ~2 hours/document), accelerating deal velocity
  * Finance: Improving insights quality, reducing turnaround times with AI-generated reports and analytics
  * Security: Reducing time to detection, enhancing threat response with AI-generated incident reports
  * Support: Delivering real-time suggestions for ticket resolution, improving onboarding speed for new agents by 30%
## The AI-First Enterprise Journey
* AI Adoption Maturity Curve (5 stages):
  * Stage 1: AI primarily for basic information retrieval and insights (humans make all decisions/execute workflows)
  * Stage 2: AI executes predetermined workflows across departments (humans define processes/monitor outcomes)
  * Stage 3: AI systems interact to solve complex problems (humans provide strategic oversight, remain in loop)
  * Stage 4: AI begins to improve its own performance (humans approve strategic changes, manage multiple AI Agents)
  * Stage 5: AI independently identifies needs and manages entire business functions (humans set vision)
* Realistically, most enterprises are at Stage 1
* Key Questions for AI Readiness Assessment:
  * Do we have the right policies, architecture, governance, and infrastructure to support AI agents?
  * Can our data systems support intelligent decision-making?
  * Do we allow for interoperability and optionality within the AI stack?
  * Do we have a change management approach in place?
  * How will we measure the effectiveness and impact of AI adoption?
* Proactive design around these dimensions will lead to leadership in the new era of intelligent business
## Conclusion
* The journey offers a blueprint: Think beyond basic automation, reimagine workflows, prioritize trust
* Embrace AI not just as a tool, but as a transformation vector to unlock exponential impact
* Requires a company-wide reimagination and deliberate shift across every aspect of the enterprise
* Organizational shifts:
  * From fixed corporate processes to continuously self-improving systems
  * From reliance on hiring human expertise to dynamic deployment of agentic labor/AI-driven specialization
  * From long employee onboarding to instant productivity with AI assistance
  * From skill gaps limiting innovation to AI agents bringing hard-to-find capabilities on demand
* Early adopters will gain operational advantage and redefine industry standards
