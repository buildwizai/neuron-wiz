# The AI Vision of the Future: Reflections from Pioneers
## Pioneering Achievements and Recognition
- Honored with the 2025 Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering
- Recognized for singular impact on today's artificial intelligence technology
- Celebrated for pioneering achievements in advanced machine learning and AI
- Innovations are shaping modern life

## Personal "Aha" Moments & Career Turning Points
- Yoshua Bengio
 - Moment 1 (Grad Student)
  - Read Jeff Hinton's early papers
  - Thought simple principles (like physics laws) could explain human intelligence
 - Moment 2 (Post-ChatGPT)
  - Realized risks of machines understanding language and having uncontrolled goals
  - Shifted research agenda to address potential harms
- Jensen Huang (Nvidia)
 - Moment 1 (Late 90s)
  - Figuring out how to overcome the "memory wall"
  - Led to stream processing and GPU computing
 - Moment 2 (2010/2011)
  - Breakfast with Andrew Ing (Google finding cats with 16,000 CPUs)
  - Repeated experiment on 48 GPUs; convinced Nvidia should build GPUs for deep learning
  - Realized deep learning network scale was similar to scaling chip design
- Geoffrey Hinton
 - Moment (1984)
  - Tried back propagation to learn the next word in a sequence (tiny language model)
  - Discovered it could learn interesting features for word meanings
  - Principles were the same as today's big models, but lacked compute and data for 40 years
- Fei-Fei Li
 - Moment 1 (2006/2007)
  - Struggled with visual recognition and generalizability
  - Realized machines were starved of data compared to intelligent animals
  - Created ImageNet (15 million images across 22,000 categories)
  - Aha moment: Big data drives machine learning
 - Moment 2 (2018, Google Cloud)
  - Realized AI is a civilizational technology impacting every sector
  - Returned to Stanford to co-found the Human-Centered AI institute (HAI)
- Yann LeCun
 - Moment 1 (Undergrad)
  - Fascinated by training machines instead of programming them (self-organization)
  - Obsession: Training multi-layer nets (a known limitation since the 60s)
 - Moment 2 (Post-ImageNet)
  - Initially focused on supervised learning (worked well due to labeled data)
  - Refocused on self-supervised/unsupervised learning (discovering structure in data) around 2016/2017

## The Current State of AI: Demand and Valuation
- The "Bubble" Debate
 - Concerns about expectations exceeding reality and market reckoning
 - Jensen Huang's Perspective (Not a Bubble)
  - Contrast with Dot-Com era
   - Today, almost every GPU is "lit up and used" (unlike dark fiber)
  - AI requires "factories" to produce intelligence (tokens) in real time
  - Driven by two exponentials
   - Amount of computation needed is growing
   - Usage of AI models is growing
  - AI augments people and addresses labor
  - We are at the very beginning of the buildout of intelligence
 - Optimist View (No Bubble)
  - Models are becoming more efficient (e.g., GQA, MLA vs. straight attention)
  - Models are consistently getting better and will not go backwards
  - Applications have only scratched the surface (maybe 1% of ultimate demand)
 - Nuanced View (Potential Bubble within Paradigm)
  - Yann LeCun: Investment is justified for applications and infrastructure (e.g., smart wearable devices)
  - Yann LeCun: A bubble exists if the current LLM paradigm is expected to achieve human-level intelligence alone
  - Fei-Fei Li: AI is a young field (less than 70 years old) with many frontiers to conquer

## Future Directions and the Quest for AGI
- The Evolution of LLMs
 - They should not be called "Language Models" anymore
 - Now advancing as "agents" that go through interactive steps to achieve goals
 - Technology is rapidly changing (unpredictable in 2-10 years)
- New Paradigms and Challenges
 - Need scientific breakthroughs for the next generation of AI (beyond current LLMs)
 - Current LLMs fail at rudimentary spatial intelligence tests
 - Need to focus on spatial intelligence (perception and action)
 - Apply self-supervised learning to video and sensor data
 - AI researching AI (programming, algorithms) is advancing very fast and could unlock further progress
- Timeline for Human-Level Intelligence (AGI)
 - Not going to be a singular event; capabilities will expand progressively
 - Fei-Fei Li: Parts of machines already supersede human intelligence (e.g., 22,000 object recognition, translating 100 languages)
 - Jensen Huang: The technology is productive now, so the timeline is an academic question
 - Geoffrey Hinton: Machines will definitely win a debate with humans within 20 years
 - Bill Dally: Goal is to augment humans, complementing weaknesses so humans focus on creativity and empathy
 - Yann LeCun: AI ability to plan suggests AI could reach the level of an employee in engineering tasks within about five years
