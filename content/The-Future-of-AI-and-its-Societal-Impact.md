# The Future of AI and its Societal Impact
## World Beyond 2035-2050
* Rate of technological change will be difficult to frame
* Human experience may not change much
  * Biology is baked in over a long time
* What's possible in the tech stack and individual output will be very different
* Many things like Dyson spheres or nanobots are possible

## Impact on Companies and Jobs
### Demise of Incumbents
* Faster demise of Fortune 500 in 2030s than ever seen
* Most current companies will fail to adapt quickly enough
* New companies can grow bigger and more important faster (e.g., OpenAI)
### Software Industry Transformation
* Any software can be just-in-time written by AI
* Type into an AI chatbot to get great software built
* Instead of buying SAS products, you just "run and it happens"
* Physical world changes slower, but 2040 is long enough
### AI-Capable Professions (80% by 2035 and beyond in intellectual world)
* AI doctors, therapists, oncologists, structural engineers, chip designers
* AI software engineers, salespeople, marketing people, accountants
### Human-Preferred Jobs
* Many people don't want an AI to do certain jobs
* New kinds of jobs will emerge that people particularly want a person to do
* Caring about other people (deep biological thing)
  * A great AI teacher may not be as motivating as a mediocre human teacher
* More time for caring for children, elders, and family relationships

## OpenAI's Journey and Product Strategy
### Origins and Early Challenges
* Started as a research lab, then bolted on a product company (unusual)
* Became clear they needed to build a big company due to scaling laws and capital
* GPT3 initially not good enough for a product
* Paul Graham's advice: always make an API
### GPT3 API and User Discoveries
* Crowdsourced GPT3 via API to the world
* The only thing that made money with GPT3 were copywriting applications (e.g., Jasper)
* The "playground" (testing prompts) showed clear user signal that people wanted to chat with models
### Evolution to ChatGPT
* Research focused on making models easier to chat with (no complicated prompts, better models)
* Decided to put out a chat interface, despite internal concerns about whether people would chat about "nothing"
* Early preview: low retention, but for users who did retain, usage increased over time
  * Learning: If a product has any retention at all (e.g., 5%), you're in good shape
### Vision for OpenAI Products
* Build a small suite of products and a platform
* Be the default personal AGI
  * System that will get to know you, connected to your stuff, behaves as you want
* Flexible interfaces: chat, social products, entertainment products, work with agents
* Log into any service and bring your intelligence there
* Create great experiences and integrate in different ways
* Current state is "terminal stage of AI"; goal is to build the next modern computer interface
* Vision: ChatGPT as an "operating system for intelligence"

## Drivers of AI Progress (Scaling Laws)
* At a high level, inputs haven't changed much
* Better algorithms
  * Keep finding steeper and steeper scaling laws
  * Algorithmic progress has been incredible (reasoning, unsupervised learning)
* Bigger computers
  * More powerful chips, connecting more of them together
* More and better data
* Continuous learning systems
  * Systems that run forever and get smarter and smarter
* AI will assist humans in research
  * AI generating 10-30% of PRs, starting to test model architectures
  * Researchers output 2x or 10x more than before
* Faster rate of progress on the research front every year
  * AI helps build data centers faster, develop new chips faster
* Key test: AI coming up with new hypotheses, testing, and modifying them itself

## AI in Enterprise
### Virtual Co-workers
* AI will make all enterprise tasks better
* Examples: coding agent stuff, customer support with AI agents
* Proportion of work done by AI versus people will go up and up
### Solving Hard Problems
* Enterprises will throw entire clusters of compute at really hard problems
* Scientific discovery is a most exciting application
* Discovering new materials, optimizing very difficult supply chains
### Short-term Disruption and Revenue
* The AI software engineer will be the most disruptive thing to enterprises
* Companies are investing the most in this, fastest
* Directly translates to a current limiting reagent and revenue opportunity for most companies
### Traditional Enterprise Functions
* Customer support, outbound sales, other verticals are already using AI

## Challenges and Decisions at OpenAI
* Hardest thing: The sheer number of decisions and tasks that interrelate and must be done at once
* No good Silicon Valley wisdom for making many decisions quickly
* Struggling to transform OpenAI into an organization that makes only a few big decisions
* Difficulty of doing something new instead of copying competitors
* General case of ChatGPT being really good at therapy and coaching has surprised many people

## Advice for Entrepreneurs
* Assume that models are going to get 10x better every year on roughly every dimension
  * Better, cheaper, more multimodality, better intelligence, better reasoning, lower costs
* Don't try to outsmart it; ask: "If an oracle tells me this, what product should I build when?"
* Impressed by current generation of AI application founders
* A 10-person company with $1 billion in revenue: either already started or will in the next couple of years
  * Examples: AI for science (drug discovery), entertainment

## Global Implications of AI
* Addressing the worry of "the rich get richer"
* ChatGPT could become the biggest website in the world, with billions using free AGI
* Universal access to great medical advice, great education, and ability to get software made for free
* Technology benefits the world by putting free or low-cost tools in the hands of people
* People are good at learning how to use new technology and know what they need
* AI-driven scientific discoveries will create wealth for founders but provide massive global benefit
  * Cures for every cancer (cheap cures for all)
  * Commercial fusion (plummeting electricity prices for all)
* Technology on the whole spreads massive global benefit
* Concerns: Compute could become a crazy scarce resource, access could be limited
* Solution: Make way, way more compute
* Should be a force towards more equality in the world, not further divergence
* AI will cause a hugely deflationary economy in the 2030s
* Excess wealth will need to go somewhere; hope basic needs become affordable for everyone
  * Water, food, healthcare, education, access to nature, time with family

## Energy Infrastructure for AI
* Models will get hugely more efficient
* There is so much demand that many gigawatts (tens, eventually hundreds) of energy will be needed
* Optimistic about new energy sources coming online that will be abundant and cheap
* AI and energy are tremendously related things
* Expect the cost of AI to converge to the cost of electricity in 10 years

## Role of Government in AI
* Ensure that AI is abundant and not something only accessible by rich people
* Need for guardrails: avoiding overregulation (disaster) and underregulation (bad)
* Setting global rules, especially as systems get much more powerful (enforced by governments)
* Questions about how distribution and access are shared
* Questions about data in this kind of world
* Expect AI to be one of the very major topics for politics by the US 2028 election

## Future of AI Interface and Research
### Bandwidth of Interface with Agents
* Problem: Current interfaces make it hard to deal with many agents simultaneously; need to shift interfaces, wait for reports, editing is involved
* Solution: An agent whose job is to evaluate outputs of other agents and decide what to surface
* Custom-generated UI that evolves in real-time
* AI getting smarter and working at higher levels of executive function
### Breakthroughs in Physical World and Biology
* Bottleneck: Generic vs. domain-specific models, or the data itself
* Some areas of science (e.g., physics): A smarter intelligence might make breakthroughs with no more data
* Biology: Less likely to find cures without running additional wet lab experiments
  * Need active learning systems that can keep getting new data points (e.g., AI asking a scientist to run an experiment)
